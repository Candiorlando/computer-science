## Illinois Department of Human Services — Rehabilitation Counselor Series
**Report Date:** May 17, 2026  
**Requestor Title:** Rehabilitation Counselor Senior  
**Data Source:** Illinois Comptroller Salary Database  
**Scope:** 200 unique employees across 3 job title categories

---

## Executive Summary

Males earn more on average than females in **all three job title groups** within the Rehabilitation Counselor classification series. The pay gap is largest at the **Senior level** (6.1%, or $6,090/year). The workforce is 83% female, yet males consistently cluster at the top of each pay band.

## Section 5: Key Findings & Conclusions

1. **Males earn more in every job category.** The pay gap ranges from 1.4% (Trainee) to 6.1% (Senior). The Senior-level gap represents $6,090/year in absolute terms.

2. **The gap is widest at the top.** The RC Senior group — the highest classification — shows the largest male pay advantage. Males cluster at $9,200–$9,600/month while many female Seniors occupy lower positions within the same pay band.

3. **Workforce is 83% female, yet males earn more.** Despite being a clear minority (17% of the workforce), males consistently earn above-average salaries across all three classification levels.

4. **Pay compression exists.** Two male Trainees and one male mid-level Counselor earn at or above what some RC Seniors earn — suggesting the pay band structure may not be functioning as intended, or individual step placements have not been regularly reviewed.

5. **JONES, TERRANCE (RC mid-level, $9,200/month) is the most notable anomaly.** His salary exceeds the female pay ceiling for his entire classification and surpasses several employees in the higher-classified Senior role. This warrants direct review.

6. **The highest-paid Trainee overall is female** (REESE, DEBRA, $7,800/month), demonstrating that the disparity is not universal at the individual level — but the averages consistently favor males at every tier.

## Methodology Notes

- **Records analyzed:** 200 unique employees (deduplicated by Employee ID, most recent year retained)
- **Gender determination:** No gender field exists in the dataset; gender was inferred from first names using standard name-gender classification. Ambiguous names defaulted to the statistically predominant gender.
- **Pay field:** `Rate_of_Pay` interpreted as monthly salary (consistent with Illinois state pay scales for these classifications)
- **Trainee records:** All carry Issue_Year "0," indicating a different data extraction period (likely a career-history view)
- **Data years in dataset:** 2024, 2025, 2026 (plus legacy "0" for Trainees)
